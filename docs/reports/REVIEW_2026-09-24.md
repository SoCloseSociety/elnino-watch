### Reviewed and found sound (no change)

- Scheduler / concurrency: one task per collector, `_running` guard, per-run timeout
  min(interval, 300 s), `_hooks_lock` around the risk evaluation; `replace_events` is one
  transaction and refuses to wipe on an empty fetch; SQLite is a single connection behind an
  RLock (no intra-process "database is locked"), WAL with a 30 s timeout for the verify
  script. The risk engine runs synchronously on the event loop after every collector round
  (dozens of small queries, ~50-200 ms): acceptable, noted below.
- Forecast vs observed: `/api/latest` excludes future rows and today's Open-Meteo daily rows;
  every factor / signal carries `kind` / `valid_for`; the cyclone factor reads only sources
  that use `replace_events` (GDACS, JMA, JTWC, EONET), so a dissipated storm cannot linger.
- Security in public mode: every non-GET needs the admin token (constant-time compare, fail
  closed when unset); `/api/layers/verify` admin-only; the snapshot proxy only fetches URLs
  written by our own collectors (NDBC / JMA / GOES), never a visitor's URL (no SSRF); the SPA
  route resolves inside `frontend/dist`; no user input reaches a response header; nginx
  gzips JSON and rate-limits; uvicorn runs with `--proxy-headers`, so HSTS is sent.
- Rate limits: GDELT gate (1 request / 20 s, one back-off), Reddit 1 query per run, Bluesky
  0.3 s gaps, X keyless strategies with health tracking; Open-Meteo heavy requests budgeted.

## 4. El Nino analogs (new phase)

`RONI_full.ascii.txt`).

### How it works

- **Data.** Open-Meteo archive, `models=era5`, point 9.5705 N 99.9957 E at 10 m (the cell
  9.5 N 100.0 E, the same as the live `openmeteo_samui_era5` and its 1991-2020 normal),
  daily rain / max temperature / max feels-like since 1950-01-01, fetched in decade windows
  (weight ~261 Open-Meteo "calls" each, one window per hourly run, none in the first 5 min
  after start-up; the full history costs ~2,100 calls once, the current year ~20 a day)
  and reduced to a monthly table (rain, day count, maxima, means, days >= 39 °C feels-like,
  dry days, dry-run bookkeeping across month ends) in status `samui_era5_history`
  (73 KB, 921 months). Monthly rain and maxima are also observation series
  (`samui_era5_month_precip`, `_temp_max`, `_apparent_max`, dated the 15th).
- **Events.** 1982-83, 1987-88, 1991-92, 1997-98, 2009-10, 2015-16, 2023-24; peak ONI /
  RONI read from series `oni` / `roni` (NOAA CPC). Verified against the DB: 1987-88 peaks at
  ONI +1.49 (ASO 1987) / RONI +1.34, i.e. **just under NOAA's "strong" band** in the current
  ONI; the page says so (`note`) and labels it "moderate El Nino". 2023-24 is strong by ONI
  (+1.99) but only +1.42 by RONI.
- **Water year** = July of the developing year to June of the next; seasons Oct-Dec
  (refill), Jan-May (dry), Feb-Apr (danger window); percentages vs the 1991-2020 monthly
  means of the same cell; heat and dry spells over Jan-May.
- **Neutral baseline** = 18 water years with ONI within +-0.5 °C from SON to FMA (1956,
  1959-62, 1978, 1980-81, 1985, 1989-90, 1993, 1996, 2001, 2003, 2012-13, 2024).
- **Impacts**: 46 entries in `analogs_impacts.py`, every URL fetched live (HTTP 200 or a
  Crossref-resolved DOI) with a verbatim quote: 26 tied to the listed events (1997-98: 3,
  2009-10: 6, 2015-16: 7, 2023-24: 10), 1 to the current event (Bangkok Post, 29 Jul 2026),
  15 documented non-El-Nino floods / storms (March 2011, Dec 2016 - Jan 2017, Pabuk Jan
  2019, Dec 2022 ferries, Nov-Dec 2024) labelled as such, 4 papers. Looked for and NOT
  found (listed in the payload as `impacts_not_found`): a Samui shortage report for
  2019-20, for 1998 and 1992-93; Samui-specific 2016 bleaching percentages; DDPM tallies
  for Pabuk; a Nov 2011 Samui flood.

Takeaways generated by the endpoint (each number is in the payload):

1. In 6 of 7 strong El Ninos, Samui's Feb-Apr rain was below 60% of the 1991-2020 normal
   (1982-83, 1991-92, 1997-98, 2009-10, 2015-16, 2023-24); the neutral-year median is 66%
   (n=18). The very strong events had 8-16%.
2. Jan-May rain was below 75% of normal in 6 of 7 events: event median 55% vs 82% in
   neutral years.
3. The Oct-Dec refill was below 80% of normal in only 3 of 7 events (1991-92, 1997-98,
   2009-10); event median 91% vs 92.5% neutral: a strong El Nino does not guarantee a weak
   NE monsoon on the Gulf coast (and the January 2016 floods with ferries halted happened
   in the very strong 2015-16 event).
4. Dry-season heat: max feels-like reached 40.3 °C in 2023-24 (27 May 2024); event median
   39.4 °C vs 37.7 °C in neutral years (cell values, cooler than a Samui thermometer).
5. Days at or above 39 °C feels-like in Jan-May: event median 1 vs neutral 0; the most was
   11 in 2023-24 (the recent events are the hottest: the warming trend).
6. Longest dry spell in Jan-May: event median 28 days vs 23 in neutral years; 50 days in
   1997-98, 45 in 2015-16.
7. At the same stage (JJA), ONI +1.80 °C in 2026 is above all 7 analogs (1997-98 +1.48,
   2015-16 +1.44 were the highest): the 2026 event started early and fast.
8. So far in 2026 (Jun-Aug, ERA5 through 18 Sep): 254 mm = 96% of normal; the decisive
   months (Oct-Dec refill, then Feb-Apr 2027) are still ahead.

Limits stated on the page and in the help topics: 7 events, one 30 km cell that under-reads
island rain and heat, a warming climate since 1982, and every El Nino different.
