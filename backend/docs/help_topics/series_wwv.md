# Warm water volume (TAO / PMEL)

id: series_wwv
short: The volume of warm water (above 20 C) stored along the equatorial Pacific, measured by the TAO buoy array.

## What it is
NOAA PMEL computes each month the volume of water warmer than 20 C above the thermocline between 5N-5S, 120E-80W, for the whole basin and its west (120E-155W) and east (155W-80W) halves, plus the average 0-300 m temperature. Series wwv_anom, wwv_west_anom, wwv_east_anom (units of 10^14 m3), wwv_total, t300_anom, t300_west_anom, t300_east_anom (degC).

## How to read it
Anomaly above 0 = more warm water than normal. Before an El Nino the warm water builds up (recharge); during the event it moves east (east half rises, west half falls) and is then discharged. August 2026: +3.5 x10^14 m3 overall, +4.0 in the east, -0.6 in the west, the classic mature El Nino pattern.

## Why it matters for Koh Samui
When the warm water volume drops below normal, El Nino is usually ending and a La Nina can follow; for Samui that is the signal that the drought-type risk will fade and a wetter year may follow.

## Limits
Monthly, based on buoys and ocean analyses; gaps in the TAO array add uncertainty.
